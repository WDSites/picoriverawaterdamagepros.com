Commercial water damage restoration starts with one phone call

Commercial water damage restoration is the drying, cleanup and repair of a non-residential building after water gets into walls, ceilings, floors or contents. A leaking suite, a flooded back room and a stained office ceiling all count.

Callers describe the building on the first call. Dispatch listens, asks a few questions, and confirms by phone whether we can take the loss. Some buildings fit and some do not, and we say so plainly. The 24/7 dispatch line stays open around the clock, and we send a licensed and insured crew when one is available.

While you wait, stop the water at its source if you can reach the shutoff, move stock and papers off the floor, and keep staff and customers away from wet areas.

What a commercial loss involves that a house does not

A shop or office rarely has one owner and one set of walls. Water from a supply line in one unit can cross a shared wall, drop through a ceiling into the unit below, or run under a flooring seam into the next space. Part of the job is finding how far it went, not just where it started.

We map moisture with meters and thermal imaging cameras across every room that touches the wet area. If the neighboring space is not yours, we tell you what we found on your side and leave the next door to its occupant and the building owner.

  • Shared walls and party walls that wick water sideways
  • Ceiling tiles and grid systems that sag, stain or drop
  • Carpet tile, vinyl plank and sealed concrete that each dry differently
  • Insulation, ductwork and wiring above the ceiling
  • Stock, files and furniture that need to be moved before drying

Who decides what: tenant, owner and landlord

Most commercial leases split the building. The owner usually carries the structure, and the tenant carries contents, improvements and the business. Your lease says where the line sits, so read it before anyone signs a work order.

We can dry and document the loss, but suppliers, customers, staff schedules and downtime are your decisions. Questions such as whether to close for a day, reroute orders or tell the landlord belong to the business owner. We give you a clear scope so you can make those calls with facts.

Safety steps before anything else

Water and live electrical equipment do not mix, and a commercial space has more of it. Counters, server closets, lighting circuits and sign power can all be wet at once. When the floor around the electrical panel is wet, stay away from it. Any electrical or gas hazard goes to the utility or 911 ahead of us.

The CDC advises against flipping power on or off or running electric tools while you are standing in water. It also recommends that equipment be checked by a qualified electrician before power is brought back. SoCalGas tells anyone who smells gas to leave right away and call from a safe place. Sewage or unknown water means stay out until it is assessed.

How we dry a commercial space

After the moisture map, extraction units pull standing water and air movers start moving air across wet surfaces. LGR dehumidifiers take moisture out of the air so the building does not simply trade wet floors for damp walls. Where water is contaminated, containment barriers and HEPA air scrubbers keep the problem in one area.

Readings are repeated until wall and floor materials come back to normal for that building. A good question for any contractor is whether the work follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published industry standard, and which final readings you will be shown.

What happens next is driven by three things: the category of the water, the length of time the material stayed wet and its makeup. Clean supply-line water treats differently than gray or contaminated water, and we explain the category before work starts.

Records for your insurer and your landlord

Both your insurer and your landlord will want proof of what happened. We record moisture readings, photograph affected areas and keep a written scope of what was removed and why. Add your own photos of damaged stock and equipment before anything is moved.

The California Department of Insurance guide notes that flood is typically excluded from standard commercial policies, though wind-driven rain damage may be covered. Coverage for a supply line failure, roof leak or flood turns on the wording of your policy, so put the question to your insurer and agent directly. We never promise coverage.

What changes the cost of a commercial loss

There is no honest number before someone sees the building. Cost is driven mainly by how clean the water is, how much floor and wall area is affected, what the building is made of, how long drying and equipment take, and how much rebuild follows.

Occupied space adds scheduling questions, such as working around business hours or keeping a customer entrance open. The real figure comes only after the loss is seen. Before any work begins, we walk through the scope and the price.

Business license, permits and what the city says

The city's General Plan economic chapter says a business license is required for anyone doing business in Pico Rivera. When you hire any trade for repair work, ask to see its license and confirm it is current for work in the city.

Per the city's permitting FAQ, interior demolition counts as permit work. Remodel-level electrical and plumbing usually needs one too, and finish-only swaps like flooring are usually exempt. If anything is unclear, ask the Building Division before tearing out a ceiling or wall.

Water loss mistakes to avoid in a business

The costliest mistake is waiting to see if a stain dries on its own. Wet material left in a closed suite over a weekend keeps spreading behind finishes you cannot see.

Other common errors include running the HVAC over wet ceilings, throwing away damaged items before photographing them, and assuming a neighbor's landlord will handle a shared wall. Call your own insurer early, and keep every receipt.
