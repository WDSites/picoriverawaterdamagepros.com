Storm Water Damage Cleanup After a Heavy Rain

Storm water damage cleanup is the removal of rainwater that entered a home during or after a storm, followed by drying the floors, walls and contents it touched. It covers rain pushed sideways through windows and doors, overflow from gutters, and runoff that ran toward the house across the yard. Our licensed and insured crews take calls on the 24/7 dispatch line.

If water is on the floor now, stay off wet carpet near outlets, lift small items to a dry surface and put towels along the door or window where it is coming in. Do not wade through standing water that is touching electrical outlets or appliances.

Four Ways a Rainstorm Gets Into the House

Most storm losses come from a short list of paths. Wind-driven rain forces water through the gaps around window frames, sliding doors and garage doors. Clogged gutters and downspouts spill at the wall instead of carrying water away. Soil that slopes toward the house sends runoff to the foundation, and a blocked yard drain gives water nowhere to go.

If the rain came through the roof, ceiling or an attic, that is a different job, and our page on roof leak water damage explains it. This page is about water that comes in at the sides and the ground level.

Pico Rivera Storm Season and Local Drainage

Storm season, as LA County Public Works sets it, covers October 15 through April 15. Use that window to clear gutters, check the slope beside your foundation and stock supplies before the first big system arrives.

A county drainage assessment, cited in the city's General Plan, turned up a handful of places with localized flooding or poor drainage. Two of them are Greenvale at Masoncrest Drive and Olympic north of Acacia Avenue. Anyone living close to either should watch how water moves past the property during a storm and ask a licensed contractor about grading or a drain.

Storm Safety Steps Before You Clean Anything

The National Weather Service says six inches of fast-moving water can knock an adult off their feet and twelve inches can carry away most cars. It also says it is never safe to drive or walk into floodwater, so do not go outside to check a drain during a storm.

Stay out of any room where water reaches outlets, cords or the electrical panel. Cut power to that area only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. Otherwise call the utility or 911 before calling us, and never run a vacuum or space heater while standing on wet floor.

Sandbags, Alerts and Getting Ready Before the Next System

LA County says sandbags are available at county fire stations and that you should call first to check. Sand can be bought at home improvement stores, and people generally must live in the community where they request sandbags. Place them at door thresholds and low garage openings, not against the wall.

The city's emergency management page says alerts go out through the city website, local radio and Alert LA County. Sign up before the season starts, so you hear about a heavy storm before it arrives.

How We Remove Storm Water and Dry the Structure

We begin with extraction units to pull standing water off hard floors and out of carpet, then check how far it wicked using moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera. Baseboards are often pulled so air can reach the wall cavity behind them.

Next come air movers and LGR dehumidifiers set to match the room, with readings taken until the materials return to a dry range. Gritty runoff water may be category 2 or worse, and we treat it that way. Carpet pad that stayed wet is usually removed, and our water damage cleanup work covers the contents and surfaces.

What Changes the Cost After a Storm

Cost depends on how much water entered, its category, which rooms and materials it reached, the number of days drying equipment must run and the rebuild that follows. A wet strip by one door and a garage that took runoff are very different jobs.

No honest figure exists until someone has seen the damage, and we lay out scope and cost for you before any work begins. Photograph everything first, so the scope reflects what actually happened.

Insurance, Flood Coverage and Wind-Driven Rain

The California Department of Insurance guide notes that wind-driven rain damage may be covered under standard homeowners, renters and commercial policies, whereas flood is typically excluded. Which one applies to your loss depends on how the water got in, so describe it accurately and check your policy.

FEMA's floodsmart site says nearly one third of NFIP claims from 2014 to 2024 came from outside high-risk flood areas, and flood policies typically take effect after a 30-day waiting period. Look up your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and talk with your agent about coverage long before a storm. Water that rises from outside is covered in our flood damage restoration page.

Mistakes to Avoid During and After a Storm

The most common mistake is waiting for the carpet to dry on its own while the wall behind the baseboard stays wet. Another is running a household fan across a soaked room and spreading muddy water residue through the house.

Do not drive through flooded streets to reach supplies, and do not throw out damaged items before you photograph them. Throw away wet items that cannot be cleaned and dried, per EPA guidance for homes after disasters.
