Crawl Space Water Damage: Water Beneath a Raised Floor

Crawl space water damage is standing or seeping water in the shallow area under a raised floor, along with the wet insulation, subfloor and framing it touches. If you can hear a pump, see water in the vent opening, or smell damp earth, the 24/7 dispatch line is open. Shut off the water at the main if a pipe is leaking, and stay out of the crawl space until power is dealt with.

Does Your Home Have a Crawl Space?

Raised foundations exist across Southern California, but no source we reviewed counts crawl spaces in Pico Rivera. We cannot say how many homes here have one. If your home has a crawl space, you will usually find a small access hatch in a closet floor, a hallway, or an exterior panel.

If you do not see one, your home may sit on a slab, and our slab leak page covers that case. A plumber, inspector or your own records can confirm the foundation type in a few minutes.

Where Crawl Space Water Comes From

There are two broad sources. Plumbing leaks from supply lines, drains or a water heater can run down into the space and pool on the soil. Drainage problems let rain, irrigation or surface runoff flow toward the foundation and in through vents or low spots.

Storm season in LA County, as Public Works defines it, begins October 15 and ends April 15. Use that stretch to walk your yard and trace where roof runoff goes. Downspouts that empty beside the foundation, soil sloping toward the house and clogged yard drains can all send water under a raised floor.

Safety Before Anyone Goes Under the House

A crawl space mixes water, wiring and tight quarters. Do not crawl in while water is standing near electrical boxes, outlets or conduit. Per the CDC, you should not turn power on or off yourself when you are standing in water. Federal workplace safety guidance from OSHA says the same about damp ground: stay out and leave electrical equipment alone.

If you smell gas, leave right away and call from outside. SoCalGas says not to flip switches, not to smoke, and never to repair a pipe yourself. Phone the utility or 911 before anything else, and contact us afterward. If water came from a drain or sewer line, treat it as contaminated and keep out.

How We Work in a Tight Space

Access is the first challenge. Crews often enter through a hatch and work on their backs or knees, so the job takes planning. We find the water with moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras, then use extraction units and pumps to remove standing water from the soil surface.

Wet insulation is pulled out and bagged, since it holds water against the subfloor and does not dry well. Air movers are positioned to push air across the underside of the floor, and LGR dehumidifiers pull the moisture from the air. Ducting runs the equipment out through a vent or hatch.

A vapor barrier is a plastic sheet laid on the soil to slow ground moisture. We check whether it is torn, shifted or missing, and we explain what we see so you can decide about repairing it.

What Changes the Cost

The drivers are how much water is present, how much insulation and subfloor is wet, how hard the space is to reach, and the number of drying-equipment days that the moisture readings require. A leak from a clean supply line differs from contaminated water, which adds removal and disinfection.

The real number comes after someone sees the loss. Before any work begins, the crew walks through what the job includes and how the cost is set. If demolition or plumbing work is needed, the city says interior demolition and plumbing work often need a permit, so ask the Building Division.

Insurance Fit

The California Department of Insurance says flood falls outside homeowners coverage, though other kinds of water damage may be covered. Rising groundwater or runoff after a storm may be treated as flood, while a sudden pipe failure is often treated differently. Read your policy and ask your insurer which one applies.

Keep photos of the crawl space before anything is removed, save receipts, and keep a claim diary. Hold off on permanent repairs until the adjuster has inspected the damage.

Mistakes to Avoid and How to Prevent a Repeat

  • Closing the hatch and hoping the water drains on its own
  • Running a household fan down the hatch, which can spread moisture and spores
  • Leaving wet insulation in place because the floor above looks fine
  • Entering a space with standing water near wiring
  • Adding a new floor over a subfloor that has not been checked
