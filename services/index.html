How the services fit together

A burst supply line under a kitchen sink is a good example. Extraction removes the standing water, mitigation dries the cabinet base and the floor beneath it, cleanup removes the swollen particleboard and any wet insulation, and repair puts the cabinet and flooring back. Each step has a different crew skill and a different set of tools, which is why the pages are separate.

A flooded garage after a heavy storm follows a different path. Flood damage restoration treats the water as possibly contaminated from the start, so more materials come out and more of the work is sanitizing. Our write-up for water damage cleanup explains what gets removed and what gets saved.

None of these services is waterproofing. Restoration deals with damage that already happened. If water keeps coming in through a foundation or a drainage problem, a waterproofing or drainage contractor is the right trade for that part of the job.

Mold remediation is a separate step

Mold needs moisture, so it usually follows a leak that sat, a ceiling stain nobody tracked down, or a drying job that stopped too early. Mold remediation starts with finding and fixing the water source, then containing the area, removing affected material and confirming the result. Ask any company whether its crews follow the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation.

Where mold shows up after a recent leak, drying the structure properly first is part of the answer, and the water mitigation page explains how that works.

What decides the price

No service page quotes a number, because the number depends on the loss. Five factors move it: the water category, how much area is affected, which materials are involved, how many days of drying equipment the job needs, and how much rebuilding follows. Scope and price are explained before work starts, after someone has seen the damage.

General insurance note: sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by a homeowners policy, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

What to do before help arrives

Whichever service you need, a few safe steps help right away.

  • Shut off the main water valve if you can reach it.
  • Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and safe to reach, and call 911 or the utility for any electrical or gas risk.
  • Lift valuables off wet floors and take photos before moving anything.
  • Keep people and pets out of water that may be contaminated.
