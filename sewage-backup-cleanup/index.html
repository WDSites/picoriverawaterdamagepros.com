Sewage Backup Cleanup: What It Means and What to Do First

Sewage backup cleanup is the removal, containment and disinfection of wastewater that has come up out of a toilet, tub, floor drain or sink. The 24/7 dispatch line is open if this is happening now. Until a crew arrives, stop flushing and running water, close doors to the affected rooms, and keep children and pets away.

Why Sewage Is Treated as the Worst Category of Water

Restoration work sorts water into three categories. Category 1 comes from a clean supply line, category 2 is used water such as a washing machine overflow, and category 3 is grossly contaminated water. Sewage is category 3 every time, even when it looks fairly clear and even when it is mixed with rainwater.

That label changes the job. Crews wear protective gear, seal off the affected area, and treat porous materials very differently than they would after a pipe leak. A wet couch from a supply line may dry out. A couch touched by sewage usually does not.

Whose Pipe Is Blocked: The Lateral or the City Main

The pipe that runs from your house to the public sewer is called a lateral. LA County Public Works and the LA County Sanitation Districts both say the lateral belongs to the property owner it serves, including the stretch under the street or in an easement. Clearing roots and grease from it is the owner's job.

The City's Sewer Division, according to the city's utilities page, runs the sewer mains and lift stations inside city limits, while regional trunk lines and treatment belong to the LA County Sanitation Districts. A blockage in either place can push waste back into a home. If other houses on your street are also backing up, call the city. If only your fixtures are affected, call a plumber to inspect the lateral.

First-Hour Safety Steps Before We Arrive

The CDC notes that floodwater can carry sewage and that its contents are unknown. If you must enter the room, it recommends rubber boots, rubber gloves and goggles, and washing your hands after contact. Do not walk the contamination into clean rooms on your shoes.

Electricity is the other danger. According to the CDC, you should never switch power on or off while you are standing in water. OSHA adds that wet areas should be avoided and electrical equipment left untouched when the ground is wet. If the panel sits in a flooded space, contact the utility or 911 before you contact us.

Do not use bleach on carpet, do not run a household vacuum over sewage, and do not try to dry the area with a fan. Fans spread contaminated moisture through the house.

Our Cleanup Process: Containment, Removal, Disinfection

We start by sealing the affected area with plastic sheeting and containment barriers so contamination stays put. Extraction units remove the liquid, and moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras show how far it traveled under baseboards and into wall cavities.

Porous materials that sewage has soaked into are cut out and bagged. That usually includes carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation. The CDC says items that cannot be disinfected, such as drywall, rugs and wall coverings, should be thrown away.

Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and treated with a disinfectant. Then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry what remains, and HEPA air scrubbers run under negative air so airborne particles are filtered out rather than pushed into the rest of the home.

Who Handles the Pipe and Who Handles the Cleanup

We do not repair sewer lines. A licensed plumber clears the blockage or replaces the damaged section, and the city handles a problem in the public main. We handle everything the water touched inside and around the home.

The Pico Rivera municipal code lists sewage or grease overflowing from clogged sewer or building drain lines as a public nuisance. That is one more reason to get the line fixed and the cleanup done promptly. If you rent, tell your landlord right away, because the lateral and the cleanup bill may not fall on you.

If a plumber is already on the way, you can call us in the same hour. We coordinate around their access to the drain.

What Changes the Cost of Sewage Cleanup

Several things set the price of the work, and none of them can be known over the phone.

  • How much area the sewage reached and how far it spread under flooring
  • How much porous material has to be removed and replaced
  • Whether it is a short overflow or a long, slow seep into walls
  • How many days of drying and air scrubbing are needed
  • Whether rebuilding is needed after the demolition

The real number comes after someone sees the loss. Scope and price are explained before work starts. Interior demolition may need a city permit, and the city Building Division can tell you what applies.

Insurance and Sewage Backups

Homeowners policies generally exclude flood but may cover other kinds of water damage, according to the California Department of Insurance. Whether a sewer backup is covered depends on the exact wording of your policy, so read it and ask your insurer directly. We cannot say whether a claim will be approved.

Take photos, keep every receipt, and write down each conversation with the insurer. Make only the temporary repairs needed to stop more damage until the adjuster has seen it.

Mistakes to Avoid After a Backup

  • Running the toilet or the tap to see if the line has cleared
  • Pouring chemical drain openers into a line that is already full
  • Cleaning with a mop and bucket and leaving wet carpet in place
  • Waiting for the smell to fade before calling
  • Pulling out wet drywall by hand without gloves or eye protection

Sewage that is allowed to sit soaks deeper into wood and drywall. Mold can follow when material stays wet, and our mold remediation team handles that if it comes up later.
