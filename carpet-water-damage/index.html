Carpet Water Damage Restoration, Explained

Carpet water damage restoration is the process of extracting water from carpet and pad, drying the layers beneath them, and deciding what can stay and what must go. Carpet is only the top layer. Under it sits a cushion pad, then tack strip around the edges, then the subfloor, and each one reacts differently to water. A restoration crew treats the whole stack, not just the visible surface.

Clean Water, Gray Water and Dirty Water

The source of the water sets the plan. A supply line or a rain-fed roof leak starts as clean water, but it can turn dirtier as it soaks through dust, old stains and pad. Water from a washer drain or dishwasher is a step worse, and sewage or floodwater is the worst case.

Tell dispatch exactly where the water came from. That one detail decides whether we plan to dry the carpet or plan to take it out.

Carpet Versus Pad: Why They Get Different Answers

Carpet fibers are often synthetic and handle water fairly well. Pad is foam or fiber cushion that acts like a sponge, holds water long after the carpet feels dry on top and is cheap compared with the labor to dry it. Many jobs end with the original carpet lifted, cleaned and dried while the pad is replaced.

That is why a quote may mention pad separately. Ask whether the plan keeps the carpet, replaces the pad, or does both.

The city's permitting FAQ lists flooring among non-structural cosmetic changes that generally do not need a permit. If you are replacing carpet after a loss, that is useful to know, but confirm with the city Building Division if the job grows to include walls or other work.

Census QuickFacts puts owner occupancy in Pico Rivera at 70.5 percent, which leaves roughly three in ten homes occupied by someone other than the owner. Renters should tell the landlord about wet carpet promptly and check what your renter's policy says about belongings.

What a Wet Carpet Tells You

You may see a dark stain along the wall, ripples across the room, or a damp feel when you step on it. Some people notice a sour smell first. Use the table as a rough guide until readings are taken.

Safety First With Wet Carpet

Wet carpet conducts electricity from nearby outlets and extension cords, so keep power tools and appliances away from it. Cut power to the area only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Call the utility or 911 before calling us if there are sparks, a burning smell or gas.

CDC flood guidance says floodwater may contain sewage and its contents are unknown. Anything that cannot be disinfected, rugs and wall coverings among them, should be discarded, it adds. Wear boots and gloves if you must walk through it, and keep children and pets out.

Drying Carpet in Place or Floating It

When the water is clean and the carpet is caught quickly, a crew can often dry it where it lies. A weighted extraction tool pulls water out of the fibers, and then air movers and dehumidifiers dry the room. Moisture meters check the pad and subfloor.

Floating is the other method. The crew releases the carpet from the tack strip at one wall, lifts it, and props it on a pad or rack so air can move underneath. The original pad is usually discarded and the subfloor is dried while the carpet hangs. The carpet is restretched afterward.

In its guide on mold after disasters, the EPA advises drying out the house and belongings quickly and throwing away floodwater-soaked items that cannot be cleaned and dried in that window.

Odor, Tack Strip and the Subfloor

Odor is a sign that something stayed wet. Removing the pad and drying the subfloor usually ends it, while deodorizing a carpet over a wet floor does not. Tack strips are wooden strips with sharp pins, and they can swell, stain or rust, so they are often replaced when the carpet is reinstalled.

If the subfloor is damp or darkened, it needs readings and sometimes removal of a section. The structural drying page explains how the floor under the carpet is dried.

What Changes the Cost

Price moves with the water category, how much carpet is affected and whether pad and tack strip get replaced. Drying time and equipment, plus any subfloor work or new carpet, also move the total. A small clean-water spill and a large contaminated one are not priced alike.

A firm figure is only possible once someone has looked at the damage. Before any work begins, the scope and the price are laid out for you.

Insurance and Carpet Claims

Flood is not covered by homeowners policies, the California Department of Insurance says, but other kinds of water damage may be. A sudden, accidental leak is often covered, and a slow one often is not. Photograph the carpet before anything is lifted and keep every receipt.

Mistakes to Avoid With Wet Carpet

The most common mistake is running a fan over wet carpet and calling it done while the pad underneath stays soaked. Another is steam cleaning carpet that was soaked by sewage, which only spreads contamination. Whether a company's crews follow ANSI/IICRC S500, a published industry standard covering water damage restoration, is a fair question to put to any firm.

Do not lay a new pad on a subfloor you have not tested.
