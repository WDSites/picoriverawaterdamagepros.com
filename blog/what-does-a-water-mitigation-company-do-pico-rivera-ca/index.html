What Does a Water Mitigation Company Do in Pico Rivera?

By the Pico Rivera Water Damage Pros team · Updated October 27, 2026

A step-by-step look at what happens on a mitigation job, from the first visit to the final moisture reading.

☎ Call (562) 271-5850

A water mitigation company stops water damage from spreading by removing the water, drying the structure and documenting what happened. It is the emergency phase of the job, and it comes before any rebuilding. If you are comparing providers, this is the work a water mitigation crew should walk you through.

Below is the sequence on a typical job, written so you know what to expect and what to ask. Details change with the size of the loss and the type of water.

Step 1: The call and the first assessment

The first conversation is about safety and the source. The dispatcher asks what happened, whether the water is still running, whether power or sewage is involved and what you can safely do while you wait. Close the main valve if a pipe broke, and stay out of rooms where water meets electricity.

On site, the technician walks every affected room, looks for the source and checks the water category. Clean, gray and contaminated water are handled differently, so this early decision shapes the plan. You should get a plain explanation of what the crew sees before any equipment is set.

Step 2: Moisture mapping

Water travels farther than it looks. Moisture meters read dampness in walls, floors and baseboards, and thermal imaging cameras show cooler, wet patches behind surfaces. The crew marks affected areas and records readings so later visits can be compared against the first.

Mapping decides the scope. It shows which materials can be dried in place and which are too wet or too contaminated to keep. It also gives you a record of how far the water spread.

Step 3: Extraction

Standing water goes first. Extraction units and pumps clear the floor, and wands pull water from carpet and pad. Removing bulk water mechanically is far faster than letting it evaporate.

Materials that cannot be saved come out at this stage. That can include soaked pad, wet insulation or drywall that has wicked water upward. Removing them early gives air a path into wall cavities and lowers the drying load.

Step 4: Drying with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers

Drying is a system, not a single machine. Air movers push air across wet surfaces so moisture leaves the material. LGR dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air so it does not settle back onto walls and floors.

Air movers

Compact blowers placed at angles along walls and over floors. They speed evaporation from surfaces and open cavities.

LGR dehumidifiers

Low grain refrigerant units that remove moisture from the air. They keep humidity in the room low so drying keeps moving.

Specialty tools

Injection drying systems, drying mats and HEPA air scrubbers may be added when the building or the water type calls for them.

People often ask whether they can rent this equipment themselves. Equipment placement and monitoring are the hard parts, and household fans and a store dehumidifier rarely match a plan built from moisture readings.

Step 5: Monitoring visits

A good job includes return visits. The technician checks readings, adjusts equipment and decides whether any material needs to come out after all. Drying takes days, and the meters tell the story, not the calendar.

Equipment comes out when readings reach the dry standard set at the start. If a company removes equipment without showing you numbers, ask to see them.

Step 6: Documentation

Photos, moisture readings, equipment logs and a written scope make up the paper trail. Those records show what was done and why, and they help if you file a claim. Keep your own photos too, along with receipts and a claim diary of every call.

Do not make permanent repairs until your adjuster has seen the damage. Mitigation is temporary work meant to prevent further damage, so it fits what insurers generally expect before rebuilding starts.

Mitigation, restoration and repair: what is the difference?

These words get mixed up, and the mix-up can cause confusion over quotes. The table separates them by purpose, so you can see which phase a quote covers.

PhasePurposeTypical work
MitigationStop damage from spreadingExtraction, moisture mapping, drying equipment, removal of unsalvageable materials
RestorationReturn the space to usable conditionCleaning, sanitizing, drying to standard, content handling and some rebuild planning
RepairRebuild what was removedNew drywall, insulation, flooring, paint and trim

One company can do all three, and the rebuild phase is covered under water damage repair. Either way, the scope and price for each should appear separately. That makes it easier to compare bids and to review the work with your insurer.

What to ask before you hire

  • Do your crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration?
  • Will you map moisture and share the readings with me?
  • What equipment will be used, and how will you monitor it?
  • How is the scope and price explained before work starts?
  • Which parts of this quote are mitigation and which are rebuilding?
  • Will I receive photos and drying logs?

A company that answers these plainly is easier to trust than one that rushes you toward a signature.

Cost and insurance, kept general

Mitigation pricing reflects the water category, the reach of the loss, the materials involved and the equipment days. No honest number exists until someone sees the loss, and scope and price are explained before work starts.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by homeowners policies, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer. Crews serving Pico Rivera also respond for water damage restoration in Downey and Santa Fe Springs.

Pico Rivera note: when the work needs a city permit

The city Building Division handles building permits in Pico Rivera. Electrical or plumbing work almost always needs one, and interior demolition or structural changes likely do too. Non-structural cosmetic changes usually do not. Before your crew cuts into walls or touches plumbing, ask who is responsible for permits.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Building Division about anything that does not fit those categories. The city also says express residential plumbing permits require an active California contractor license and a City business license. Ask any plumbing contractor for those details before you sign.
