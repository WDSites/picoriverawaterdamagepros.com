Slab Leak Water Damage: Where It Starts and What We Do

Slab leak water damage happens when a pipe running in or under a concrete slab foundation leaks and the water works its way up into flooring, baseboards and lower walls. The 24/7 dispatch line is open if you suspect one now. While you wait, shut off the main water valve if the meter is still moving, and keep furniture and rugs off wet floors.

Does This Page Apply to Your Home?

Only homes built on a concrete slab can have a slab leak. We do not claim to know how common slab foundations are in Pico Rivera, because no source we reviewed counts them. Many homes here may have raised floors or other foundations, and this page does not apply to those.

If you are not sure, look at your foundation or check your records. A plumber or inspector can tell you quickly. If the floor is wet and you have a raised foundation, our crawl space page covers that case, and our burst pipe page covers the more common visible leaks.

Using Your Bill and Meter as Clues

Three water providers serve parts of Pico Rivera, according to the city, and roughly 70 percent of the city is on the city's own system. Your bill names your provider, and the city offers a find-your-provider tool if you are unsure. Homes on residential accounts are invoiced every second month, which lets a leak run for weeks before the bill reveals it.

The city directs high-bill questions to its Water Billing office. Before you do, shut off every fixture and look at the meter. If the dial or low-flow indicator still moves, water is escaping, and a plumber should check the lines before the damage spreads.

Safety Steps Before the Plumber and Before Us

Pressurized water under a slab can undermine flooring and soak wiring near the floor. If outlets, power strips or the panel are near wet areas, do not touch them. The CDC warns against touching a breaker or switch while your feet are in water, so wait on dry footing or call for help. OSHA likewise tells people to keep clear of wet areas if electrical equipment is exposed.

If a hot water line is leaking, the floor and the water may be hot enough to scald. If you smell gas from a water heater or appliance, leave immediately and call from outside. SoCalGas says not to switch lights or appliances on or off and never to repair a pipe yourself.

Who Does What: The Plumber and Our Crew

When a leak is isolated to your own home, the city's FAQ tells you to hire a plumber and pay for the work yourself. Water problems that affect a whole neighborhood go to the city's Water Division, and after hours the city has an emergency water answering service. That split matters here because we do not locate or repair the pipe.

A licensed plumber uses leak detection to pinpoint the line, then repairs or reroutes it. Tell the plumber to leave access open, because we need to see the same wet floor they do. We can start drying once the water is off, even if the pipe repair is scheduled for later.

How We Dry and Restore the Floor Above the Slab

Our crew starts with moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to trace how far water has traveled from the leak point. Concrete holds moisture for a long time, so readings guide how long drying runs. Wet baseboards, carpet pad and damaged underlayment come out when they cannot be dried.

Dry air is pushed over the surfaces by air movers, while LGR dehumidifiers remove moisture from the room's air. On hardwood or laminate, we may use drying mats or panels to pull water from the boards. We keep checking readings each visit until the material reaches the dry standard for the type of flooring.

What Changes the Cost

Cost follows the damage, not the pipe. The main factors are how much floor area is wet, what the flooring is made of, how many days of drying equipment the readings call for, and whether baseboards or lower walls need to come out. Demolition and rebuild add to the scope.

The real number comes after someone sees the loss. Before any work begins, we explain the scope and the cost. The plumber's slab repair is billed separately from our work.

Insurance and Slab Leaks

The California Department of Insurance says sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. A slab leak can be either, and the policy wording and the plumber's findings both matter. Ask your insurer which parts of the loss they cover, and whether access to the pipe is included.

Take pictures, keep receipts, and note every call with the insurer. Temporary measures that stop additional damage are fine, but wait for the adjuster to see the damage before making permanent repairs. We cannot promise approval.

Mistakes to Avoid With a Suspected Slab Leak

  • Ignoring a high bill because the floor still looks fine
  • Covering a damp spot with new flooring or a rug
  • Running a space heater or fan on a hot floor to dry it
  • Waiting to call a plumber until the carpet is soaked
  • Replacing flooring before the leak is found and repaired
